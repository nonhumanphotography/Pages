Welcome: Keep What Works

Lesson 7 shifts from full generation to selective revision. Your mission is to keep what works, repair what fails, expand what is missing, and document each decision.

Core learning question: What should I preserve, what should I change, and how can I use partial generation without losing the parts that already work?

Learning Objectives

  1. Explain the difference between full image generation and partial generation.
  2. Define inpainting, outpainting, masking, seam quality, and prompt-based image editing.
  3. Use inpainting to correct errors, remove unwanted elements, or replace selected parts of an image.
  4. Use outpainting to expand an image beyond its original frame while maintaining style, lighting, and perspective.
  5. Decide when partial generation is more appropriate than regenerating an entire image.
  6. Write effective prompts for inpainting and outpainting tasks.
  7. Evaluate whether edited or expanded areas match the original image.
  8. Document a partial-generation workflow using prompts, masks, iterations, and critical evaluation.

Partial Generation as Selective Revision

Partial generation means generating, repairing, replacing, or extending only part of an image instead of creating a whole new image. The professional shift is selective revision: keep successful areas and change only what needs repair, replacement, or expansion.

Professional rule: Do not start over if you can repair. But do not repair endlessly if the core image, meaning, or ethics are wrong.

Inpainting, Masks, and Local Editing

Inpainting edits a selected region inside an existing image. The selected region is defined by a mask. The mask is not only a technical tool; it is a design boundary: it tells the system where it may change the image and what must remain protected.

Mask strategy: A useful mask usually covers the problem area plus a small margin of surrounding context. Too small may not give the model enough structure; too large may change successful areas unnecessarily.

Outpainting, Seam Quality, and Workflow Evaluation

Outpainting extends an image beyond its original borders. It is useful when the image needs a wider frame, a different aspect ratio, more background, or empty space for typography. The generated area must continue the original image logic.

Seam quality rule: A successful outpainting result makes the boundary between original and generated content invisible or visually acceptable.
